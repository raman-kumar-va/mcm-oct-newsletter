The Deferred Maintenance Ghost 👻

How Small Repairs Turn Into Major Expenses

Deferred maintenance rarely happens out of neglect. It often begins with decisions that seem reasonable at the time, but can leave HOAs facing much bigger expenses down the road.

Why Deferred Maintenance Happens in HOAs

Artificially Low Dues
Boards under pressure to keep assessments low may cut preventative maintenance spending to balance operating budgets.
  
Underfunded Reserves
Outdated reserve studies or insufficient annual contributions can leave associations without the funds needed for major repairs.
 
Lack of Regular Inspections
Problems with roofs, balconies, drainage, and pipes can go unnoticed until visible damage occurs.
  
Frequent Board Turnover
Changes in board leadership can cause maintenance plans and important decisions to fall through the cracks.

How a $250 Repair Becomes a $35,500 Nightmare

During a routine walkthrough, a board notices a small crack in the caulking around a second-story balcony door. The repair would cost just $250, but the board postpones it to conserve funds for seasonal landscaping.

Over the next two rainy seasons, water seeps behind the damaged caulking, eventually causing extensive dry rot in the balcony's structural framing. Two years later, water staining inside the home reveals a much bigger problem.

The HOA now faces:
Structural engineer and scope plans $3,500
Emergency balcony reconstruction $28,000
Interior mold remediation and drywall repair $4,000
Total repair cost: $35,500
142× A $250 maintenance task has become a repair scenario costing 142 times the original estimate, with additional disruption for residents.

How HOAs Can Prevent It

1
 Stay ahead of repairs: Address maintenance issues before they escalate.
2
 Plan for the future: Keep reserve studies current and budget for major components.
3
 Inspect regularly: Identify hidden problems before they cause significant damage.
4
 Document everything: Maintain clear records so projects remain on track, even when board members change.
 
The Takeaway

Good HOA management isn't about eliminating every surprise. It's about identifying potential problems early and having a plan before they become emergencies.

The Boardroom

The Ghost of Poor Documentation

When Missing Financial Records Become an HOA Nightmare

Good governance depends on accurate, accessible records. When an HOA fails to maintain proper financial documentation, something as routine as filing annual tax returns can become a serious problem.

Even though HOAs are generally organized as nonprofit corporations, they still have annual federal tax filing obligations, typically using IRS Form 1120-H or Form 1120, along with applicable state filings.

What Happens When Records Go Missing?

💸
Financial Penalties and Unexpected Tax Costs
Without complete financial records, such as monthly income statements, an HOA may struggle to prepare and file its tax returns on time. Late filing can result in penalties and interest on unpaid taxes. Missing documentation can also complicate the association's ability to report income correctly and apply the appropriate tax treatment.
⚖️
Corporate Status and Legal Complications
In California, failure to meet applicable tax and corporate filing requirements can put an HOA's corporate status at risk. Suspension or forfeiture may affect the association's ability to pursue legal claims, defend itself in court, and enforce its rights.
🏘️
Disruptions to HOA Operations
Incomplete records and unresolved compliance issues can complicate loan applications, major capital projects, and property transactions. They can also create disputes among homeowners and raise questions about whether the board has properly fulfilled its governance responsibilities.

How Boards Can Prevent This

✓ Maintain complete financial records: Keep monthly income statements, expense reports, bank statements, and supporting documents organized.
✓ Establish a filing calendar: Track federal and state deadlines and work with a qualified tax professional.
✓ Ensure continuity: Make sure records are accessible to authorized board members when leadership changes.
✓ Review compliance regularly: Confirm that tax filings and corporate requirements are up to date.
 
The Takeaway

Poor documentation doesn't just create paperwork problems. It can expose an HOA to financial penalties, legal complications, and operational setbacks.

When it comes to HOA governance, the records you maintain today can protect your community from the nightmares of tomorrow.

Seasonal Checklist

No Tricks, Just Checks! 🎃

Your HOA's Halloween Readiness Checklist

Keep your community safe, welcoming, and Halloween-ready with these simple checks before the spooky season begins.

✓
 Check outdoor lighting around walkways, entrances, and common areas to help prevent trips and falls.
✓
 Clear fallen leaves and debris from sidewalks, paths, and storm drains.
✓
 Inspect walkways for trip hazards and remove obstructions.
✓
 Review HOA rules for seasonal decorations, including placement and removal timelines.
✓
 Make sure Halloween displays don't block entrances, sidewalks, signs, or visibility.
✓
 Check gates, entryways, and shared spaces for maintenance or safety concerns.
✓
 Confirm any community event guidelines before planning Halloween activities.

A little preparation now can keep small scares from becoming bigger problems later.

Quick Knowledge

California HOA Facts & Myths

Think you know your HOA rights? Let’s separate fact from fiction.

🎃 Myth 1
“HOA boards can keep all their records private.”
Fact
California homeowners generally have the right to inspect specified HOA records, including certain financial records, tax returns, and approved meeting minutes, subject to applicable procedures and exceptions.
👻 Myth 2
“Homeowners aren’t allowed to attend HOA board meetings.”
Fact
Homeowners generally can attend open board meetings and speak during designated homeowner forums. Executive sessions are treated differently under California law.
🕸️ Myth 3
“An HOA can enforce any rule it wants.”
Fact
HOA rules must comply with applicable laws and governing documents. A rule may be challenged if it is legally unreasonable or otherwise unenforceable.

🎃 The Takeaway: Knowing your rights and responsibilities helps build a more informed, transparent community.

What’s Really Scary? 👻
Q
 Can an HOA Increase Monthly Assessments Without Homeowner Approval?
A

Yes, but California law sets limits.

Under California Civil Code § 5605, HOA boards can generally increase regular assessments by up to 20% without a homeowner vote, provided required budget-report rules are met. Larger increases generally require member approval.

20%
Regular assessments
5%
Special assessments

Special assessments exceeding 5% of the association’s budgeted annual expenses generally also require approval, subject to specific exceptions.

 
💡 The MCM Takeaway

Before questioning a dues increase, review the HOA’s notice, budget, and governing documents to understand whether the proper procedures were followed.
